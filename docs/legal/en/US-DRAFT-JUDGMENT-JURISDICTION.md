## 1. Is there a US forum at all?
1. **Subject-matter jurisdiction (federal court)**
   - Federal question (28 U.S.C. § 1331) – e.g. copyright claims arise under the Copyright Act (17 U.S.C.), exclusive federal jurisdiction (28 U.S.C. § 1338).
   - Diversity (28 U.S.C. § 1332) – citizens of different states / foreign party, amount in controversy > $75,000.
2. **Personal jurisdiction** over the defendant – state long-arm statute + Due Process ("minimum contacts"; for foreign defendants possibly Fed. R. Civ. P. 4(k)(2)).
3. **Venue** – 28 U.S.C. § 1391; for copyright 28 U.S.C. § 1400(a).
4. **Standing / registration** – US works need registration before suing for infringement (17 U.S.C. § 411(a)); foreign Berne works are exempt from the pre-suit registration requirement, but statutory damages/fees depend on timely registration (§ 412). *Verify with counsel.*

## 3. Judgments – recognition across borders
- A **US judgment** is enforced in other US states via full faith and credit / Uniform Enforcement of Foreign Judgments Act (state-adopted).
- A **foreign (e.g. Czech/EU) judgment** in the US: state law, often the Uniform Foreign-Country Money Judgments Recognition Act (2005) – not automatic, requires a recognition action.
- A **US judgment in the Czech Republic/EU**: no EU–US treaty; recognition under Czech Act No. 91/2012 Coll. (private international law), reciprocity required. *Verify with Czech counsel.*
- Hague Choice of Court Convention (2005) – the US signed but has **not ratified** (status to verify); Hague Judgments Convention (2019) – check current US status.
